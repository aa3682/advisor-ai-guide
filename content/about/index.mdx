# About this guide

This page explains what the guide is for, how it is put together, and where its limits are. It also tells you how to report a mistake and what you may do with the material.

## What this is

This is a free, public reference on using AI inside a small independent investment advisory practice. It is written for the adviser running that practice first and for the adviser at a larger firm second, so every page starts from plain English and marks the larger-firm angle where it appears.

The site is organized by The Adoption Path at the top level, in the order the work actually follows. Beneath that sit the Firm Functions the steps draw on, and a set of tools that make the work concrete.

## What it is not

This guide is educational material about how small advisory firms can use AI tools. It is not legal, compliance, tax, or investment advice, and it does not create an advisory or professional relationship. Rules vary by regulator and by state, and both the rules and the tools change over time. Verify anything here against the current requirements of your own regulator, and get your own counsel before acting.

## How figures are handled

The body pages on this site never print a limit, threshold, rate, or deadline set by law, regulation, or an agency. Those numbers change, and a figure printed in the middle of a paragraph goes stale without anyone noticing. Instead, a page names the concept in plain words and links to the one page that holds the number.

That page is [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures). Every figure there sits in its own row with the year it applies to and a link to the primary source it was taken from. The page is checked against its sources once a year.

## How sources are chosen

When a page needs support for a claim, it cites a public primary source rather than a secondary summary: statutes and regulations, agency publications, standards bodies, and academic papers cited by their DOI. Commentary, marketing material, and other people's guides are not used as sources. Where a page rests on judgment rather than a source, it says so.

## Who writes it

Written by someone with more than fifteen years of hands-on experience inside wealth management and investment advisory, working from public primary sources.

All of the writing is original, and no page reproduces text from any textbook, study guide, or publication.

## Corrections

If you find an error, a broken link, or a figure that is out of date, report it on the repository's [Issues page](https://github.com/aa3682/advisor-ai-guide/issues). Say which page and which line, and if you can, link the source that shows the correct fact.
