# Glossary

This page defines every term the guide links to, in plain English and for education rather than advice. Any number a term depends on, such as a limit, rate, or age, lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) rather than here.

### Adopting release [#adopting-release]

The document the SEC publishes when it adopts a final rule, setting out the Commission's reasoning and how it means the rule to work. Where the rule text itself is terse, the adopting release is usually where the thinking behind it is spelled out.

### Advertisement [#advertisement]

Under the [Marketing Rule](#marketing-rule), a communication to more than one person that offers the adviser's advisory services, along with any [testimonial](#testimonial) or [endorsement](#endorsement) the adviser pays for. What the content says decides the question, not what produced it. See [Client Communication and Marketing](/domains/client-communication-and-marketing).

### Advisor-specific platform [#advisor-specific-platform]

A tool built for the advisory industry, where the vendor has usually already handled data handling, recordkeeping, or supervision on the firm's behalf. The guide's default scenario assumes general-purpose tools instead, and flags where an advisor-specific platform changes the answer. See [Vet and Choose Tools](/process/vet-and-choose-tools) for weighing one against a general-purpose tool used under a signed agreement.

### Automation platform [#automation-platform]

A tool that connects the firm's other systems and acts on its own, rather than drafting something a person then sends. It sits between those systems by design, so it touches nearly everything the firm handles rather than one workflow, and it moves information out of the firm the same way typing into a chat box does. See [Operations and Back Office](/domains/operations-and-back-office).

### Books and records [#books-and-records]

The records an adviser is required to make and keep, and the rule that specifies them. The obligation runs to producing a record on request in a form the firm can furnish, not only to holding it somewhere. How long each kind must be kept is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#records-and-documentation).

### Breach notification [#breach-notification]

Telling the people whose information was exposed, and in some cases a regulator, that it happened. [Regulation S-P's safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) requires notice to individuals whose sensitive information was accessed without authorization; state breach-notification requirements also exist and vary from state to state, and a state-registered firm is under the FTC Safeguards Rule as well. Any deadline attached to a notice is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#data-privacy-and-security).

### Brochure [#brochure]

The firm's own client-facing disclosure document, describing how the practice works, what it charges, and the conflicts it carries. Whether AI use is significant enough to describe there is a disclosure question rather than a technology one.

### Broker-dealer [#broker-dealer]

A firm in the business of buying and selling securities, regulated under a separate rulebook from an investment adviser's. An adviser associated with one is a dual registrant.

### Compliance program [#compliance-program]

The written policies and procedures a firm maintains, together with the review of them and the named person responsible for administering them. AI use belongs inside the program rather than in a document beside it, or the program ends up describing a practice the firm no longer runs. How often the review must happen is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#compliance-and-supervision).

### Concentration risk [#concentration-risk]

The exposure created by routing many of the firm's workflows through one tool, so that a single vendor's outage, price change, or terms change becomes a firm-wide failure rather than a single-workflow one. The small-firm pattern makes it likely, because one capable tool tends to absorb every task it plausibly fits rather than being chosen task by task. See [Operations and Back Office](/domains/operations-and-back-office) and [Vendor Oversight](/domains/vendor-oversight).

### De-identification [#de-identification]

Removing the details that tie information to a particular client before it goes into a tool. Removing a name is not the same as removing the person: a combination of age, town, employer, and approximate account size can identify someone almost as surely as a name would, especially in a small practice. It is a real safeguard, not a complete one — see [Data Privacy and Security](/domains/data-privacy-and-security).

### Dual registrant [#dual-registrant]

An adviser who is also a registered representative of a [broker-dealer](#broker-dealer), and so works under two rulebooks with two supervisors. A use that clears the adviser's own duties can still need the broker-dealer's sign-off, and the two sets of procedures do not automatically match.

### Endorsement [#endorsement]

A statement by someone other than a current client that approves of or recommends the adviser, describes their experience with it, or solicits or refers business to it. The Marketing Rule permits endorsements only with specific conditions attached, including disclosure and disqualification, and those conditions do not relax because a tool assembled the words.

### Examination [#examination]

A regulator's review of a firm's books, records, and practices. Most of what an examiner asks for is what the firm should already be able to produce; working out an answer for the first time during the exam is the sign that something earlier was skipped.

### Failure to supervise [#failure-to-supervise]

A finding that a firm did not reasonably supervise someone who broke the securities laws. A statutory [safe harbor](#safe-harbor) protects a firm that adopted procedures reasonably designed to prevent and detect violations, had a system for applying them, and reasonably discharged its responsibilities under them without reason to believe they were not being followed.

### Fiduciary duty [#fiduciary-duty]

Under the Investment Advisers Act, an adviser's fiduciary duty means acting in the client's best interest at all times. It has two parts: a duty of care, which means giving advice that is suitable and well-researched, and a duty of loyalty, which means avoiding or disclosing conflicts of interest. Using an AI tool to help produce advice does not shift this duty elsewhere — the adviser still owes it to the client.

### General-purpose tool [#general-purpose-tool]

An AI tool that was not built for the advisory industry — a chat assistant, a document tool, or an automation platform used by any kind of business. The guide's default scenario assumes the firm is using these rather than advisor-specific vendor products, since that is the more common starting point for a small firm. What one may be given depends on the data tier and the terms it runs under; see [Sort Your Data](/process/sort-your-data).

### Hallucination [#hallucination]

A hallucination is output from an AI tool that sounds plausible and confident but is factually wrong — a fact attributed to the wrong source, a citation that does not exist, or a number invented rather than looked up. See [Prompting and Review Skills](/domains/prompting-and-review-skills) for how to catch one before it reaches a client.

### Incident response program [#incident-response-program]

The written plan for what the firm does when client information is exposed. [Regulation S-P's safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) requires one for advisers registered with the Commission, including notice to affected individuals; a state-registered firm is under the FTC Safeguards Rule, along with its state's requirements. The realistic case in a small firm is not an intruder but a paste — an authorized person putting client information into an unauthorized tool — which a plan written entirely around attackers will not recognize as an incident; see [Data Privacy and Security](/domains/data-privacy-and-security).

### Marketing Rule [#marketing-rule]

The SEC rule governing advertisements by advisers registered with the Commission, including the conditions attached to testimonials and endorsements. Nothing in it turns on how the content was produced, so an AI-drafted advertisement is treated exactly like one written by hand. A state-registered adviser is under the state's own advertising requirements instead.

### Model [#model]

The trained system underneath an AI tool, which produces the output the tool shows. The firm deals with the product rather than the model directly, and the model behind a product can be replaced, upgraded, or retired without the firm installing or accepting anything — which is why a decision to adopt a tool starts aging as soon as it is made. See [Vendor Oversight](/domains/vendor-oversight).

### Nonpublic personal information [#nonpublic-personal-information]

Regulation S-P's term for the personal financial information about individual customers that it protects; the [safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) covers records containing it. The guide uses its own plain-language tier, client-identifying, for the same idea, and cites the rule as one illustration of the concept rather than as the source of the category.

### Prompt [#prompt]

What the firm types into an AI tool to get output back — the instruction, plus whatever material the tool is given to work with. What goes into one is a data question settled by the matrix in [Sort Your Data](/process/sort-your-data) rather than a matter of convenience. Supplying the rule, the client's facts, or the firm's template keeps the work inside material the firm can check, while asking the tool what a rule requires invites it to produce knowledge from a store the firm cannot audit — see [Prompting and Review Skills](/domains/prompting-and-review-skills).

### Prompt template [#prompt-template]

A written, reusable prompt for a recurring approved use, kept where the whole firm can find it rather than in one person's head. It does more than save time: it is where the data-handling rule from the matrix gets baked in structurally, since a field reading "client-identifying details: none" is harder to violate by accident than a blank box. See [Build the Workflows](/process/build-the-workflows).

### Reasonably designed [#reasonably-designed]

The standard a firm's policies and procedures have to meet: designed to prevent violations, and judged by whether the firm actually operates them. A strict policy the firm does not follow fails this standard in a way a thinner one that is followed does not.

### Registered representative [#registered-representative]

A person registered to conduct securities business through a broker-dealer. An adviser who is also one is a dual registrant.

### Regulation S-P [#regulation-s-p]

The SEC regulation covering the privacy and safeguarding of client information, and where the [safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) sits. It applies to advisers registered with the Commission; for safeguarding client information, a state-registered adviser falls instead under the FTC Safeguards Rule, along with its state's requirements.

### Risk alert [#risk-alert]

A publication from the SEC's examination staff describing what examiners have been finding in a particular area. It does not create new rules, and it is a direct signal of where examination attention is currently directed.

### Safeguards rule [#safeguards-rule]

Regulation S-P's rule requiring written policies and procedures to protect client records and information, including an incident response program and notice to individuals whose sensitive information was accessed without authorization. Any deadline attached to that notice is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#data-privacy-and-security). It applies to advisers registered with the Commission; a state-registered adviser falls instead under the FTC Safeguards Rule, along with its state's requirements.

### Safe harbor [#safe-harbor]

A provision that protects a firm from liability where it meets the conditions the provision states. The statutory safe harbor against failure to supervise turns on whether the firm operated its procedures, not on how demanding they looked on paper.

### SEC-registered adviser [#sec-registered-adviser]

An adviser registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission rather than with a state. The SEC rules this guide cites apply to this adviser; the guide's default reader is a state-registered adviser.

### Service provider [#service-provider]

A vendor engaged to handle the firm's data on its behalf, usually under a signed agreement rather than a public terms page. Engaging one does not move the firm's own duty: under [Regulation S-P's safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule), where customer information is compromised, the duty to ensure affected individuals are notified stays with the firm — notwithstanding the use of a service provider, and notwithstanding any written agreement for that provider to give the notice on the firm's behalf. See [Vendor Oversight](/domains/vendor-oversight).

### Shadow IT [#shadow-it]

A tool someone at the firm starts using without it having been reviewed or approved. An adviser who starts using a personal transcription tool because the approved one is worse is not making a tooling choice; they are making an unreviewed vendor decision on the firm's behalf. See [Operations and Back Office](/domains/operations-and-back-office).

### State-registered adviser [#state-registered-adviser]

An adviser registered with one or more states rather than with the SEC, and the reader this guide assumes throughout. Where a page cites an SEC rule, a state-registered firm may fall outside it and under its own state's requirements, which vary; for safeguarding client information, the FTC Safeguards Rule applies in place of Regulation S-P's safeguards rule.

### Subprocessor [#subprocessor]

A downstream party a vendor passes the firm's data to — a separate model provider, an infrastructure provider, or a service the firm never chose and cannot name. Subprocessors inherit access to whatever the firm sends, on terms the firm negotiated with someone else, so oversight means knowing whether the chain is disclosed at all and noticing when it changes. See [Vendor Oversight](/domains/vendor-oversight).

### Substantiation [#substantiation]

Under the [Marketing Rule](#marketing-rule), an [advertisement](#advertisement) may not include a material statement of fact unless the adviser has a reasonable basis for believing it can substantiate that statement if the SEC asks. A claim a tool drafted needs the same basis as one written by hand.

### Suitability [#suitability]

Whether advice fits the particular client it is given to. It sits inside the duty of care described under [fiduciary duty](#fiduciary-duty), and it reaches consistency as well: similarly situated clients should get advice that reflects the same reasoning.

### Testimonial [#testimonial]

A statement by a current client about their experience with the adviser, or one that solicits or refers business to it. The Marketing Rule permits testimonials only with specific conditions attached, including disclosure and disqualification.

### Training [#training]

Using what is typed into a tool to improve the model underneath it. It differs in kind from storage: stored information sits somewhere specific and can in principle be located, deleted, or produced on request, while information used to train folds into a model's weights, where it cannot be retrieved, deleted, or audited — including by the firm that sent it. A deletion request does not unwind training that already happened, which is why this is the question to settle before adoption rather than after; see [Data Privacy and Security](/domains/data-privacy-and-security).
