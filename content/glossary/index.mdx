# Glossary

This page defines every term the guide links to, in plain English and for education rather than advice. Any number a term depends on, such as a limit, rate, or age, lives on [This year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures) rather than here.

### Adopting release [#adopting-release]

The document the SEC publishes when it adopts a final rule, setting out the Commission's reasoning and how it means the rule to work. Where the rule text itself is terse, the adopting release is usually where the thinking behind it is spelled out.

### Advertisement [#advertisement]

Under the [Marketing Rule](#marketing-rule), a communication to more than one person that offers the adviser's advisory services, along with any [testimonial](#testimonial) or [endorsement](#endorsement) the adviser pays for. What the content says decides the question, not what produced it. See [Client Communication and Marketing](/domains/client-communication-and-marketing).

### Books and records [#books-and-records]

The records an adviser is required to make and keep, and the rule that specifies them. The obligation runs to producing a record on request in a form the firm can furnish, not only to holding it somewhere. How long each kind must be kept is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#records-and-documentation).

### Brochure [#brochure]

The firm's own client-facing disclosure document, describing how the practice works, what it charges, and the conflicts it carries. Whether AI use is significant enough to describe there is a disclosure question rather than a technology one.

### Broker-dealer [#broker-dealer]

A firm in the business of buying and selling securities, regulated under a separate rulebook from an investment adviser's. An adviser associated with one is a [dual registrant](#dual-registrant).

### Compliance program [#compliance-program]

The written policies and procedures a firm maintains, together with the review of them and the named person responsible for administering them. AI use belongs inside the program rather than in a document beside it, or the program ends up describing a practice the firm no longer runs. How often the review must happen is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#compliance-and-supervision).

### Dual registrant [#dual-registrant]

An adviser who is also a registered representative of a [broker-dealer](#broker-dealer), and so works under two rulebooks with two supervisors. A use that clears the adviser's own duties can still need the broker-dealer's sign-off, and the two sets of procedures do not automatically match.

### Endorsement [#endorsement]

A statement by someone other than a current client that approves of or recommends the adviser, describes their experience with it, or solicits or refers business to it. The Marketing Rule permits endorsements only with specific disclosure and disqualification conditions attached, and those conditions do not relax because a tool assembled the words.

### Examination [#examination]

A regulator's review of a firm's books, records, and practices. Most of what an examiner asks for is what the firm should already be able to produce; working out an answer for the first time during the exam is the sign that something earlier was skipped.

### Failure to supervise [#failure-to-supervise]

A finding that a firm did not reasonably supervise someone who broke the securities laws. A statutory [safe harbor](#safe-harbor) protects a firm that adopted procedures reasonably designed to prevent and detect violations, had a system for applying them, and reasonably discharged its responsibilities under them without reason to believe they were not being followed.

### Fiduciary duty [#fiduciary-duty]

Under the Investment Advisers Act, an adviser's fiduciary duty means acting in the client's best interest at all times. It has two parts: a duty of care, which means giving advice that is suitable and well-researched, and a duty of loyalty, which means avoiding or disclosing conflicts of interest. Using an AI tool to help produce advice does not shift this duty elsewhere — the adviser still owes it to the client.

### Hallucination [#hallucination]

A hallucination is output from an AI tool that sounds plausible and confident but is factually wrong — a fact attributed to the wrong source, a citation that does not exist, or a number invented rather than looked up. See [Prompting and Review Skills](/domains/prompting-and-review-skills) for how to catch one before it reaches a client.

### Marketing Rule [#marketing-rule]

The SEC rule governing advertisements by advisers registered with the Commission, including the conditions attached to testimonials and endorsements. Nothing in it turns on how the content was produced, so an AI-drafted advertisement is treated exactly like one written by hand. A state-registered adviser is under the state's own advertising requirements instead.

### Nonpublic personal information [#nonpublic-personal-information]

Regulation S-P's term for the personal financial information about individual customers that it protects; the [safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) covers records containing it. The guide uses its own plain-language tier, client-identifying, for the same idea, and cites the rule as one illustration of the concept rather than as the source of the category.

### Reasonably designed [#reasonably-designed]

The standard a firm's policies and procedures have to meet: designed to prevent violations, and judged by whether the firm actually operates them. A strict policy the firm does not follow fails this standard in a way a thinner one that is followed does not.

### Registered representative [#registered-representative]

A person registered to conduct securities business through a broker-dealer. An adviser who is also one is a [dual registrant](#dual-registrant).

### Regulation S-P [#regulation-s-p]

The SEC regulation covering the privacy and safeguarding of client information, and where the [safeguards rule](#safeguards-rule) sits. It applies to advisers registered with the Commission; a state-registered firm is under its own state's requirements.

### Risk alert [#risk-alert]

A publication from the SEC's examination staff describing what examiners have been finding in a particular area. It does not create new rules, and it is a direct signal of where examination attention is currently directed.

### Safeguards rule [#safeguards-rule]

The SEC rule requiring written policies and procedures to protect client records and information, including an incident response program and notice to individuals whose sensitive information was accessed without authorization. Any deadline attached to that notice is a figure — see [this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#data-privacy-and-security). It applies to advisers registered with the Commission; a state-registered firm is under its own state's requirements.

### Safe harbor [#safe-harbor]

A provision that protects a firm from liability where it meets the conditions the provision states. The statutory safe harbor against [failure to supervise](#failure-to-supervise) turns on whether the firm operated its procedures, not on how demanding they looked on paper.

### SEC-registered adviser [#sec-registered-adviser]

An adviser registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission rather than with a state. The SEC rules this guide cites apply to this adviser; the guide's default reader is a [state-registered adviser](#state-registered-adviser).

### State-registered adviser [#state-registered-adviser]

An adviser registered with one or more states rather than with the SEC, and the reader this guide assumes throughout. Where a page cites a federal rule, a state-registered firm is under its own state's requirements, which vary.

### Substantiation [#substantiation]

Under the [Marketing Rule](#marketing-rule), an [advertisement](#advertisement) may not include a material statement of fact unless the adviser has a reasonable basis for believing it can substantiate that statement if the SEC asks. A claim a tool drafted needs the same basis as one written by hand.

### Suitability [#suitability]

Whether advice fits the particular client it is given to. It sits inside the duty of care described under [fiduciary duty](#fiduciary-duty), and it reaches consistency as well: similarly situated clients should get advice that reflects the same reasoning.

### Testimonial [#testimonial]

A statement by a current client about their experience with the adviser, or one that solicits or refers business to it. The Marketing Rule permits testimonials only with specific disclosure and disqualification conditions, and a quote a tool assembled from several client comments is still one.
