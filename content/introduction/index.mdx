# Introduction

This guide explains how to use AI inside a small independent investment advisory practice, from the first step of The Adoption Path to the ongoing work that keeps it current. It is free, public, and written in plain English.

## Who this guide is for

This guide is for the adviser who already runs a small independent practice — a solo registered investment adviser, or one with a partner or an assistant — and wants to use AI in the work of the firm without creating a compliance problem. It assumes the firm exists and has a [compliance program](/glossary#compliance-program) in place, however thin; the question here is how AI fits into a practice that is already running, not whether to start one. A second audience is the adviser at a larger firm who wants to understand what independent practitioners are doing with these tools, and what the rules actually require, before raising it internally.

## The scenario this guide uses

Most of this guide follows one default scenario: a [state-registered adviser](/glossary#state-registered-adviser) with no staff beyond an assistant, using [general-purpose AI tools](/glossary#general-purpose-tool) — chat assistants, document tools, and [automation platforms](/glossary#automation-platform) that were not built for the advisory industry — rather than advisor-specific vendor products. That is the more common starting point for a small firm, and it keeps the guide concrete. Where an [advisor-specific platform](/glossary#advisor-specific-platform) changes the answer, usually because the vendor has already handled data handling, recordkeeping, or supervision on the firm's behalf, the relevant page says so and flags the divergence rather than assuming the general-purpose path throughout.

## Where this comes from

This guide is built from public primary sources — statutes, regulations, agency releases, and standards documents — read alongside more than fifteen years of hands-on experience inside wealth management and investment advisory. It is not a first-hand account of running this process inside any one firm; it is a synthesis of what the rules require and what the available tools actually do, written so an independent adviser can act on it.

## How to use this guide

The Adoption Path is a sequence: it has a start, an end, and a point where it is done. Firm Functions are standing bodies of knowledge: they do not finish, because compliance, data handling, and vendor oversight are ongoing concerns for as long as the firm operates. Steps link out to the functions that apply rather than repeating that material, so each fact lives in one place.

If you are new to the subject, read [The Adoption Path](/process) in order. Each step builds on the one before it. If you have a specific question, go straight to the relevant [Firm Function](/domains). Every area page stands on its own.

<Callout type="info">
This guide is educational material about how small advisory firms can use AI tools. It is not legal, compliance, tax, or investment advice, and it does not create an advisory or professional relationship. Rules vary by regulator and by state, and both the rules and the tools change over time. Verify anything here against the current requirements of your own regulator, and get your own counsel before acting.
