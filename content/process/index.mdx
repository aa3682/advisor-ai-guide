# The Adoption Path

Adopting AI inside a small advisory practice follows a repeatable sequence rather than a string of one-off decisions. This section walks through that sequence step by step, with one page for each.

## Why a sequence

Decisions made in isolation tend to work against each other: a firm that picks a tool before it knows what the data-handling rules require often has to unwind that choice later. A fixed order makes sure nothing is skipped, and each step produces exactly what the next one needs.

## The steps

1. [Decide What AI Is For](/process/decide-what-ai-is-for): Name the problems worth solving with AI before looking at any tool, so the search stays bounded by actual firm needs.
2. [Map the Rules That Apply](/process/map-the-rules-that-apply): Work out which obligations — state, SEC, and, for [dual registrants](/glossary#dual-registrant), FINRA — bear on the uses identified in step one.
3. [Sort Your Data](/process/sort-your-data): Classify what the firm holds, from public marketing copy to client account data, so later choices about tools and workflows are grounded in what is actually at risk.
4. [Vet and Choose Tools](/process/vet-and-choose-tools): Evaluate specific platforms against the rules mapped and the data sorted, and choose the ones that fit.
5. [Write the AI Policy](/process/write-the-ai-policy): Put the firm's rules for using AI in writing, so the policy — not an individual's judgment on a given day — governs use.
6. [Build the Workflows](/process/build-the-workflows): Turn the policy into repeatable procedures for the specific tasks the firm has decided to use AI for.
7. [Handle Client-Facing Use](/process/handle-client-facing-use): Apply extra care to any use a client can see or that shapes what a client is told, where the stakes and the scrutiny are both higher.
8. [Review and Keep Records](/process/review-and-keep-records): Check that the workflows are working as intended and that the firm can show its work, on an ongoing basis.

Most firms run the first five steps — deciding, mapping, sorting, vetting, and writing the policy — before touching any client-facing use. That order is deliberate: client-facing use is the highest-stakes category, and it should sit on top of a firm that already knows its rules, its data, and its tools.

## A loop, not a line

Review and Keep Records feeds back into Decide What AI Is For. A new use case, a new tool, or a rule change reopens the sequence at the top rather than starting a separate, undocumented process.
