# Firm Functions

The Adoption Path draws on several bodies of knowledge inside the firm. Each one has its own section here, written for the independent adviser first, with the larger-firm angle marked where it appears.

## Why these areas

Every step of the process needs facts and judgment from several parts of the firm at once. Grouping that material into standing functions lets you find one topic — say, what the vendor contract needs to say — without reading the whole guide, and lets you come back to it after the Path itself is finished.

## The areas

- [Advice and Planning Work](/domains/advice-and-planning-work): How AI fits into the analysis and recommendations behind the advice a firm gives.
- [Client Communication and Marketing](/domains/client-communication-and-marketing): What changes when AI drafts or assists the words clients and prospects see.
- [Operations and Back Office](/domains/operations-and-back-office): Where AI can take on scheduling, data entry, and other internal work without touching advice.
- [Compliance and Supervision](/domains/compliance-and-supervision): How a firm supervises AI use the way it supervises any other activity.
- [Data Privacy and Security](/domains/data-privacy-and-security): What happens to client data once it reaches an AI tool, and how to keep control of it.
- [Records and Documentation](/domains/records-and-documentation): What a firm needs to keep, and for how long, to show how AI was used.
- [Vendor Oversight](/domains/vendor-oversight): How to evaluate and monitor the companies whose tools the firm relies on.
- [Prompting and Review Skills](/domains/prompting-and-review-skills): The practical skill of getting useful, checkable output from an AI tool.

Each is a standing concern rather than a step: unlike the Path, none of these areas is ever finished. A firm keeps supervising, keeps managing vendors, and keeps handling data for as long as it operates, long after any one AI tool has been adopted.
