# Writing Your AI Policy

Use this worksheet to turn the four documents the first four steps produced into one section of the compliance manual the firm already keeps. It is the companion to [Write the AI Policy](/process/write-the-ai-policy), which explains why the policy lives there and not in a document of its own; this page is where you write it down.

## What this measures

This worksheet gathers two things: the four documents you attach, and the five decisions the policy records. It produces no score — each decision has an answer and a document it points to.

The result is a policy section: a few dated pages inside the [compliance program](/glossary#compliance-program) that say what the four documents mean for day-to-day conduct, and nothing the four documents do not support.

## Before you start

Have the four documents in front of you, dated: the [use-case list](/glossary#use-case-list) from Decide What AI Is For, the [duty map](/glossary#duty-map) from Map the Rules That Apply, the [data matrix](/glossary#data-matrix) from Sort Your Data, and the tool approvals from Vet and Choose Tools. Build any you lack first; a policy written before them has nothing to point to.

Two ground rules:

- **Point, do not retype.** Every rule in the policy names the document it comes from. A rule that comes from none of the four is not a rule the firm decided, and it comes out.
- **Cover the assistant.** If the use-case list names work an assistant does, the policy covers that person, and so does the acknowledgment below.

## The documents you attach

Attach the four documents as dated appendices, in this order. Record the version you attached, because each one changes on its own trigger and the policy has to say which version it means.

| Document | Which step produced it | What you record |
|---|---|---|
| Use-case list | Decide What AI Is For | The date, and which uses are approved |
| Duty map | Map the Rules That Apply | The date, and the duties per use |
| Data matrix | Sort Your Data | The date, and which cells say no |
| Approved tools | Vet and Choose Tools | Each tool, its use, any condition, the approver, the date |

The fourth row is a list rather than one document: one line per approved tool, copied from the decision at the end of each [Vetting a Vendor checklist](/tools/vetting-a-vendor). A tool approved on a condition is listed with the condition, not as approved.

## The decisions the policy records

Work through the five decisions in this order. Each is a short answer, and each names a document.

1. **Where the policy lives.** Name the section of the compliance manual it becomes, and who keeps that manual. Not a standalone AI policy.
2. **What the policy says.** Four statements, each pointing back:
   - Who may use which tools for which approved uses — by name, from the use-case list and the approved-tools list, not "AI" in general.
   - What must never be entered into a [general-purpose tool](/glossary#general-purpose-tool) — from the data matrix, so the rule is the one already worked out.
   - Who reviews AI-assisted output before it is used inside the firm or sent to anyone outside it — a named review point, with [Handle Client-Facing Use](/process/handle-client-facing-use) linked rather than restated.
   - Who can approve a new use or a new tool — the same person Vet and Choose Tools already named.
3. **When the policy is reviewed.** Tie it to two dates the firm already has: the review step later in the Path, and the re-check trigger set for each approved tool. No third calendar.
4. **How it is communicated and acknowledged.** Everyone the policy covers, the assistant included, reads it and acknowledges that in writing, and the acknowledgments are kept with the policy.
5. **What happens when the policy is silent.** The policy says, in its own words, that a use it does not address is not allowed until someone decides; it names who decides, and the approver in decision 2 is the reasonable default.

| Decision | Which document it points to | What a clear answer names |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Where it lives | The compliance manual itself | Section, and who keeps it |
| 2. What it says | Use-case list, data matrix, approved tools | A person or a document for each of the four statements |
| 3. When it is reviewed | Approved-tools list | The review step, and each tool's re-check trigger |
| 4. Acknowledgment | The policy itself | Each person, and the date they signed |
| 5. When it is silent | Approved-tools list | The person who decides |

## Your policy section

The section is the five answers written as plain rules, followed by the four appendices. The example below is a fictional two-person firm, Harbor Lane Advisory, run by an adviser named Sam Example with one assistant; its approved-tools list holds one tool, approved for summarizing meeting notes on the condition that the notes are [de-identified](/glossary#de-identification) first.

| Provision | What Harbor Lane wrote | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | Section 9 of the compliance manual, kept by Sam | The manual |
| Who may use what | Sam and the assistant, the one approved tool, for meeting summaries only | Use-case list; approved tools |
| What never goes in | Nothing from the [client-identifying](/glossary#client-identifying) or [restricted](/glossary#restricted) rows without de-identifying first | Data matrix |
| Who reviews output | Sam, before any summary goes into a client file | Handle Client-Facing Use |
| Who approves new uses | Sam, by adding a line to the approved-tools list | Vet and Choose Tools |
| When it is reviewed | At the yearly review, and whenever the tool's re-check trigger fires | Approved tools |
| Acknowledgment | Both signed on the date the section was adopted | Signed acknowledgments |
| When it is silent | Not addressed means not allowed; Sam decides | Approved tools |

Each provision is one or two sentences. If a provision needs a paragraph, it is restating a document instead of pointing to it. Date the section on the day it is adopted, and date each appendix separately.

## What this worksheet does and does not tell you

It tells you whether every rule the firm is about to enforce can be traced to something the firm actually decided. A provision with nothing in the "Points to" column is the finding.

It does not tell you whether the four documents are right; each has its own worksheet or step for that. It does not tell you whether the section satisfies your regulator, which turns on your state's requirements or, for an [SEC-registered adviser](/glossary#sec-registered-adviser), the federal rule that the duty map already records. And it says nothing about your [fiduciary duty](/glossary#fiduciary-duty), which stays with the firm whatever the policy says.

## What to do with it

Adopt the section into the manual, collect the acknowledgments, and keep the dated set together: the section, its four appendices, and the signatures. It is the kind of record a firm may be asked to show in an [examination](/glossary#examination). Where the section or an acknowledgment is a record the firm has to keep, how long it keeps it is set by rule — see [Records and Documentation in this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#records-and-documentation) — and a [state-registered adviser](/glossary#state-registered-adviser) checks what its own state requires.

Update the section when one of its appendices changes, not on a calendar of its own: a new tool approved, a cell of the data matrix changed, or a use added to the list each mean a new dated version. Then carry the section into [Build the Workflows](/process/build-the-workflows), where each approved use gets its written procedure.

## At a larger firm

<Callout type="info">
At a larger firm the section already exists, written by compliance, and the adviser's job is to find it and fit inside it. Use this worksheet to check that fit: for each of the five decisions, find where the firm's manual answers it, and note where your own use-case list or data matrix is narrower than the firm's rule. Do not draft provisions of your own; where the manual is silent on a use you want, that is a question for whoever approves tools at the firm, not a gap you fill.
</Callout>
