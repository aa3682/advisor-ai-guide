# Vetting a Vendor

Use this checklist to check one AI tool, for one use, against the five things that decide what the firm may put into it, and to record your decision. It is the companion to [Vet and Choose Tools](/process/vet-and-choose-tools), which explains the reasoning; this page is where you write the check down.

## What this measures

This checklist looks at one tool, for one use. It does not compare vendors or produce a short list.

It produces no score. Each check has an answer and a place where that answer is written, and the result is a dated decision for this one use.

## Before you start

Have three things in front of you: the [use-case list](/glossary#use-case-list) entry this tool is meant to serve, the [duty map](/glossary#duty-map) for that use, and the cell of the [data matrix](/glossary#data-matrix) it would fill. The matrix crosses four tiers of information — public, internal, [client-identifying](/glossary#client-identifying), and [restricted](/glossary#restricted) — with four kinds of tool: the consumer tier of a [general-purpose tool](/glossary#general-purpose-tool), a paid business tier of the same tool, a tool under a signed agreement, and an [advisor-specific platform](/glossary#advisor-specific-platform). The tier sets the bar, as "Your decision" explains.

Two ground rules before you fill anything in:

- **Read the tool's own pages.** Use its terms-of-service, data-handling, and privacy pages and any agreement offered — not a summary, review site, or sales page.
- **Check it like any other tool.** An advisor-specific platform gets the same five checks, in the same order, rather than an assumption that its product settles the questions.

## What to check

Work through the five checks in this order.

| Check | Where to look | What a clear answer says |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Whether inputs are used to [train](/glossary#training) the [model](/glossary#model) | Data-handling page; plan terms; any agreement | Whether what you type, paste, or upload is used to improve the service, and whether that is a setting or a commitment |
| 2. The retention period | Data-handling page; any agreement | How long inputs and outputs are kept, and what ends that period |
| 3. Whether the vendor will sign an agreement acting as the firm's [service provider](/glossary#service-provider) | Account terms; the agreement itself | Yes or no, and what the agreement covers |
| 4. Whether [subprocessors](/glossary#subprocessor) are disclosed | A published list, or the agreement | The parties the vendor passes data to, by name or by role |
| 5. What happens to data on cancellation | Terms-of-service; any agreement | Whether you can take your data out, and whether the vendor deletes what it holds |

On the first check, a setting someone can switch back on is weaker than a commitment in the terms; write down which you found.

On the second, the period is the vendor's to set and change, so this guide prints none; read it from the vendor's current terms.

On the third, a service-provider agreement is one in which the vendor handles the firm's data on the firm's behalf. Write down whether each commitment sits in that agreement or only on a public page. For an [SEC-registered adviser](/glossary#sec-registered-adviser), [Regulation S-P](/glossary#regulation-s-p)'s [safeguards rule](/glossary#safeguards-rule) requires the firm's policies to cover oversight of a service provider, including how quickly it must report a breach to the firm ([17 CFR 248.30(a)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-248/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR83262a0bce5ffaa/section-248.30) [VERIFY]) — see [Vendor Oversight in this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#vendor-oversight). A [state-registered adviser](/glossary#state-registered-adviser) is under the FTC Safeguards Rule and its state's requirements instead.

On the fourth, a list you can read counts as disclosure. A reference to unnamed partners does not.

## What you found

For each check, write the answer as the tool's own pages state it and name the document it came from. Where a page is silent, write "not stated": that is an answer, not a blank. The example below is a fictional note-summarizing tool, checked by an adviser named Pat Example for summarizing client meetings, which is client-identifying information.

| Check | What the terms say | Where it is written |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Training | Inputs are not used to train | Signed agreement |
| 2. Retention | Kept for a stated period, then deleted | Data-handling page |
| 3. Service-provider agreement | Signed; covers this use | Signed agreement |
| 4. Subprocessors | Published list, by name | Public subprocessor page |
| 5. Cancellation | Not stated | — |

Watch the third column: "Your decision" turns on where each answer is written. Date the whole table on the day you read the pages.

## Your decision

Start from the tier in the data matrix cell. Public or internal information may go into any of the four kinds of tool. Client-identifying information needs a tool under a signed agreement that covers this use, or [de-identifying](/glossary#de-identification) before it goes in; restricted information needs that agreement and a promise that allows the use. A public terms page alone qualifies for neither. A "not stated" on any check is a question to put to the vendor before deciding, not a pass.

| Decision | When it fits | What you write down |
|---|---|---|
| Approve for this use | Every check has an answer, and the tool meets the bar for this tier | The use, the tier, the approver, the date |
| Approve with a condition | The tool clears only once something changes, such as a setting switched off or an agreement signed | The condition, and who confirms it is met |
| Decline | The tool cannot meet the bar for this tier, or the vendor will not put the answer in writing | The check that failed |

Then add two lines:

- **Who approves.** Name the person in the firm who can approve a new tool. The approval refers to the use-case list, the duty map, and the data matrix by name, not "looks fine to me."
- **When it is checked again.** Set a re-check trigger: a material change to the tool's terms, a new use proposed for it, or a fixed review interval the firm chooses — whichever comes first.

In the example, cancellation reads "not stated," so the decision is approve with a condition: get the vendor's answer in writing before any client information goes in.

## What this checklist does and does not tell you

It tells you whether this tool, on the terms you read, clears the bar the firm has already set for this one use.

It does not tell you whether the tool does the task well, or how it ranks against another tool. It says nothing about your [fiduciary duty](/glossary#fiduciary-duty) to the client, which stays with the firm whatever tool did the drafting.

And it is true only on the date you read the pages: the vendor can change its terms, subprocessors, or model without asking the firm.

## What to do with it

Date the completed checklist, sign the decision, and keep it with the firm's other working files. It is the evidence the check happened, and the kind of record a firm may be asked to show in an [examination](/glossary#examination).

Keep each dated copy rather than overwriting the last. Where the checklist is a record the firm has to keep, how long it keeps it is set by rule rather than preference — see [Records and Documentation in this year's figures](/tools/this-years-figures#records-and-documentation) — and a state-registered adviser checks what its own state requires.

If what you found puts the tool in a different category than the matrix assumed — a paid business tier with no agreement signed, for instance — correct the matrix before anyone uses the tool. Then carry the approved tool, and any condition on it, into [Write the AI Policy](/process/write-the-ai-policy).

## At a larger firm

<Callout type="info">
At a larger firm, tool approval usually runs through a vendor management or IT security process the adviser does not own. Use this checklist to prepare what you bring to that process — the use, the tier of information, and the duties it touches — not as a substitute for it. The firm may already hold a negotiated agreement with the vendor, so ask for it before reading public pages. Approval of a tool for someone else's use is not approval for yours.
</Callout>
